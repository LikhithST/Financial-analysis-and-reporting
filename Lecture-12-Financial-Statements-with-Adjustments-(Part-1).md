## 1. Why Year-End Adjustments Are Necessary

When preparing final financial statements at the end of the financial year (**31 March** under the Indian fiscal cycle), the Trial Balance only contains transactions that have already been recorded in the journal and ledger books.

However, several transactions either cross accounting periods or are recognized only at year-end:

- **Inside the Trial Balance (Single Effect):** Any item already listed inside the Trial Balance has both its debit and credit recorded. Therefore, it appears **only once** in the final accounts (either in the Trading A/c, Profit & Loss A/c, or the Balance Sheet).
- **Outside the Trial Balance / Adjustments (Dual Effect):** Any adjustment given outside the Trial Balance represents unrecorded or incomplete entries. To satisfy the **Double-Entry System** ($\text{Debit} = \text{Credit}$), every adjustment must be recorded **twice**:
  1. Once in the **Income Statement** (Trading Account or Profit & Loss Account); and
  2. Once in the **Balance Sheet** (Assets or Liabilities side).

> **The Accrual Principle:** Under **Ind AS** (Indian Accounting Standards) and **Indian GAAP**, revenues and expenses must be recognized when they are earned or incurred, not when actual cash is exchanged.

### 2.6 Adjustment 6: Depreciation on Fixed Assets

**What it means:** The permanent, continuous decrease in the monetary value of a tangible fixed asset due to wear, tear, or obsolescence.
- *Exception:* **Land** is never depreciated.
- **Example:** Building purchased for **₹20,000** with a **10-year** lifespan:
  $$\text{Annual Depreciation} = \frac{\text{₹}20,000}{10\text{ years}} = \mathbf{\text{₹}2,000\text{ per year}}$$

| **To Depreciation on Building** | — | **2,000** | | | |

#### B. Balance Sheet Presentation

| Liabilities & Capital | Inner Amount (₹) | Amount (₹) | Assets | Inner Amount (₹) | Amount (₹) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- | ---: | ---: |
| | | | **Fixed Assets:** | | |
| | | | Building | 20,000 | |
| | | | *Less:* Depreciation (10%) | (2,000) | **18,000** |

> **Replacement Fund Insight:** Depreciation is a non-cash expense. Deducting ₹2,000 from profits prevents cash from being paid out as taxes or dividends, retaining ₹20,000 over 10 years to fund a new building.

## 4. Master Summary Matrix of Adjustments

| No. | Adjustment Item | Plain-English Meaning | First Effect (Trading / P&L Account) | Second Effect (Balance Sheet) | Classification |
| :---: | --- | --- | --- | --- | :---: |
| **1** | **Closing Stock** | Goods remaining unsold at year-end | **Credit** Trading Account (Outer Column) | **Asset** (Current Assets) | Current Asset |
| **2** | **Outstanding Expenses** | Service enjoyed, but payment pending | **Debit** P&L / Trading A/c (*Add in Inner Column*) | **Liability** (Current Liabilities) | Current Liability |
| **3** | **Prepaid Expenses** | Paid cash in advance for future period | **Debit** P&L / Trading A/c (*Deduct in Inner Column*) | **Asset** (Current Assets) | Current Asset |
| **4** | **Accrued Income** | Income earned, but cash not yet received | **Credit** P&L Account (*Add in Inner Column*) | **Asset** (Current Assets) | Current Asset |
| **5** | **Income in Advance** | Cash received for future service (unearned) | **Credit** P&L Account (*Deduct in Inner Column*) | **Liability** (Current Liabilities) | Current Liability |
| **6** | **Depreciation** | Wear and tear cost on fixed assets | **Debit** P&L Account (Outer Column) | **Asset** (*Deduct in Inner Column*) | Non-Cash Expense |
