## 1. Trial Balance

A trial balance is prepared to check the arithmetical accuracy of postings made to the journal and ledger. It lists the closing debit and credit balances of all ledger accounts. When the books are correctly posted, the total of the debit column equals the total of the credit column.

### 1.2 Suspense Account When the Trial Balance Does Not Agree

If a trial balance does not tally, a **Suspense Account** is temporarily introduced for the difference so that financial statements can be prepared. The underlying error must subsequently be identified and corrected.

For example, assume the Discount A/c has a debit balance of ₹3,000 instead of ₹300. The debit total then exceeds the credit total by ₹2,700. A credit balance of ₹2,700 is placed in the Suspense Account to make the trial balance agree.

## 2. Trading and Profit and Loss Account

The Trading and Profit and Loss Account is a financial statement prepared after the trial balance. It is commonly divided into two parts:

1. The **Trading Account**, which determines gross profit or gross loss from direct income and direct expenses.
2. The **Profit and Loss Account**, which determines net profit or net loss after indirect expenses and other income are considered.

![Relationship between financial statements](Images/financial-statement(balances).png)

### 2.1 Trading Account

In a Trading Account, direct expenses are recorded on the debit side and direct income is recorded on the credit side. Sales and closing stock are the principal sources of direct income. Closing stock represents goods not yet sold; it will contribute to income in a future period.

Direct expenses are costs without which production or purchase of goods for sale would not be possible. Only direct expenses and direct income are included in the Trading Account.

Since direct income of ₹177,100 exceeds direct expenses of ₹135,400, the business earns a **gross profit of ₹41,700**. If direct expenses exceeded direct income, the balancing figure would instead be a gross loss.

### 2.2 Key Points

- Sales and closing stock are direct incomes in the Trading Account.
- Opening stock, net purchases, wages, factory rent, and freight on purchases are direct expenses in this example.
- The gross profit is carried forward to the Profit and Loss Account, where indirect expenses and other incomes are used to determine net profit or loss.
