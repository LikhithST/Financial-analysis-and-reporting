# Lecture 6: Journal (Part I)

## 1. Journal Entries

A **journal** is a book of original entry in which transactions are recorded in chronological order before they are posted to the ledger. Recording a transaction in the journal is called **journalising**.

Every transaction has a twofold effect: at least one account is debited and at least one account is credited. The total debit amount must always equal the total credit amount.

### 1.1 Format of a Journal

The journal format shown in the notes uses the following columns:

| Date | Particulars | L.F. | Debit (₹) | Credit (₹) |
| --- | --- | :---: | ---: | ---: |
| Date of the transaction | Accounts affected and narration | Ledger folio | Amount debited | Amount credited |

**L.F. (Ledger Folio)** records the page number of the ledger to which the journal entry is posted.

## 2. Traditional Rules of Debit and Credit

| Account type | Meaning | Debit rule | Credit rule |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Personal account | Accounts of persons, firms, and organisations | Debit the receiver | Credit the giver |
| Real account | Assets and property, including cash and furniture | Debit what comes in | Credit what goes out |
| Nominal account | Expenses, losses, incomes, and gains | Debit expenses and losses | Credit incomes and gains |

For goods bought for resale, the account used is **Purchases A/c**. For goods sold, the account used is **Sales A/c**. Under the traditional classification, Purchases, Sales, and Purchase Returns are **nominal accounts** because they record expenses, income, or reductions of expenses.

## 5. Points Illustrated by the Entries

### 5.1 Business Entity Concept

When the owner introduces cash into the business, the business treats the owner as a separate party. Therefore, cash is debited and the owner's capital is credited.

### 5.2 Credit Sales and Purchases

When a person's name is given in a sale or purchase transaction, it generally indicates a credit transaction:

- A credit sale to a customer creates a debit balance in that customer's personal account.
- A credit purchase from a supplier creates a credit balance in that supplier's personal account.

### 5.3 Trade Discount and Cash Discount

- **Trade discount** is deducted before recording the sale or purchase. It is not entered separately in the books. Thus, Raja is debited with the net amount of ₹6,000.
- **Cash discount** arises when a debtor or creditor settles an account for less than the balance due. Discount allowed is a loss and is debited; discount received is a gain and is credited.

For example, Naresh Kumar owed ₹8,000 but paid ₹7,600. The ₹400 difference is Discount Allowed. Vinod Kumar was owed ₹2,000 but accepted ₹1,900; the ₹100 difference is Discount Received.

### 5.4 Stationery and Rent

Stationery used for office purposes and office rent are business expenses, not goods held for resale. Their purchases are therefore recorded in their respective expense accounts rather than in Purchases A/c.

### 5.5 Purchase Returns

When goods previously bought on credit are returned to a supplier, the supplier's account is debited and **Purchase Returns A/c** is credited.
